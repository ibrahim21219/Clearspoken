In the roomFive questions

The questions we answer

Every programme is built around the situations you actually face. These are the five questions almost every client brings to the first session.

Accent + standard

How do I switch between my accent and standard English without losing who I am?

You keep your accent. We add a neutral register beside it and train the switch until it takes no thought at all.

We start by mapping what your accent does differently from standard English: usually four or five sounds and a rhythm pattern. You learn the neutral version of each, then practise switching mid-sentence until the choice is yours.

Confidence

How do I stay sure-footed in presentations and one-to-ones?

Breathing, structure and a few rehearsed openings that hold when the pressure rises.

You build a short opening for each kind of meeting you run and rehearse it until it survives nerves. A steady first thirty seconds carries the rest.

Clarity and pace

How do I keep a pace everyone can follow the first time?

A tempo people can follow, with pauses that give your point room to land.

Most fast talkers do not need to slow down everywhere. We find the two or three places where listeners lose you and put the pauses there.

Voice and persuasion

How do I use my voice to move an audience, not just inform it?

Stress, pitch and silence: the tools that turn information into conviction.

You learn to mark the one word in a sentence that carries the point, and to let silence do the rest. We practise it on your own pitch.

On camera

How do I come across naturally on video and social media?

Camera habits that let you look and sound like yourself on screen.

We record you every week and review the footage together: eye line, pace and the first ten seconds, which decide whether people keep watching.

About

Meet Nora Aldridge

I am Nora Aldridge: voice and speech specialist, conservatoire-trained singer and broadcast voice. For more than ten years I have studied how living speech lands in a room, and what stops it. I grew up with a strong regional accent and still use it every day. What I teach is the switch.

The Clearspoken method combines phonetics, breath work from classical singing and rehearsal on your own material. Nothing is drilled in the abstract: every exercise comes from a meeting, pitch or call that is already in your diary.

I spent years coaching actors and broadcasters out of their accents and back into them. Executives kept asking for the same thing, with less time and more at stake. Clearspoken is that training, rebuilt around a working week.

A client who had avoided speaking at her own company's all-hands for six years, and who now opens it. She still sounds like where she is from whenever she wants to.

Good ideas lose rooms for bad reasons. I want what you know to be judged on its merits, not on a vowel.
